About

Principal Product Designer

Enterprise UX and design systems, with recent depth in AI-agentic products — and a habit of turning ambiguous problems into patterns other teams can build on.

I design enterprise software for people whose job is to manage complexity — IT admins, security teams, and the systems they depend on. I'm at my best with the ambiguous, not-yet-defined problem — the one where "what even is this" is still an open question — and turning it into an interaction model, a set of patterns, and eventually something a whole team can build from without starting over each time.

That range has spanned building the admin experience for a brand-new product from scratch, to more recently defining what it means for AI agents to act on a user's behalf inside enterprise software — where the boundary between what an agent should do automatically and what needs a human to sign off matters as much as the feature itself.

Day to day, I work closely with PMs, engineers, researchers, and content designers, and I care about leaving teams in a better position than I found them — clearer patterns, better documentation, designers who don't need me in the room to make the right call. I also build interactive prototypes myself rather than only speccing them, because a working prototype settles arguments a deck can't.

Process

Built with AI, directed by me

I used Claude, an AI coding assistant, to build this site — I'm a designer, not a developer, and it let me move fast. But “used AI” undersells what actually happened: nothing here shipped because AI suggested it. A few examples of what “directed” meant in practice:

A single scrolling page, restructured into four
The first version was one long page with anchor-linked sections. I wanted a real Home / About / Projects / Résumé structure — separate pages, a résumé that displays in-page instead of forcing a download, a home page that doesn’t try to do everything at once — and had the whole site rebuilt around that.
The default palette was corporate navy
AI’s first pass leaned into a navy-and-white “enterprise software” look. I already had a warmer direction in mind — dusty blue and soft pink on a warm off-white — from an earlier design conversation, and redirected it there.
Copy that read “AI agents person,” not product designer
Early drafts leaned hard into AI-agent language and Microsoft-internal jargon — exactly the kind of narrow framing that can get filtered out of a broad job search. I had the home and about pages rewritten until they read as a general Principal Product Designer, with AI-agentic work as one strong thread, not the whole identity.
A layout that looked designed but didn’t mean anything
One pass color-coded a Problem/Role grid to visually echo a Before/After comparison above it. It looked intentional — it wasn’t. The pairing didn’t actually hold up conceptually, and I asked for a different pattern rather than keeping a fix that just looked finished.
What not to publish
No phone number. Colleague feedback cited without names or dates, available on request instead. Calls about what’s appropriate to put in front of strangers online — not something AI defaults to without being asked.
