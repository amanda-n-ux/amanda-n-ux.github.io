North Star

The human touch, in a world of AI

This portfolio is itself an example of a human directing AI rather than being replaced by it (see the About page's "Process" section). The visual language should make that argument, not just state it — organic and hand-touched beats geometric and templated whenever the two are in tension.

  • Organic over geometric. Wobbly, hand-drawn-feeling lines (see: Decision markers, the flower mark's petal outline) instead of straight CSS borders wherever an accent is purely decorative.
  • Imperfection is a feature. The flower mark's dots are irregular in size and placement on purpose — perfectly even spacing reads as machine-made, not hand-made.
  • Process imagery over polished screenshots. Whiteboard photos, sketches, and wireframes read as more human than clean product screenshots — lean on them wherever available.
  • Show, don't over-explain. Fewer paragraphs, more images, shorter statements pulled out and given room to breathe (see: Pull Quote).
  • When in doubt, ask "does this feel human, not templated?" That's the tiebreaker over "does this look clean/professional" if the two conflict.
Core Rule

Boxes mean clickable. Plain text means static.

A panel-fill background + border is reserved exclusively for the two components that are actual links (Explore cards, Case cards). Everything else — Decisions, Skills, Quotes, Narrative — uses typographic accents (dots, organic rules, quote marks) instead of a box, so nothing implies an affordance that isn't there.

Palette

Colors

Black/white core, two brand accent families (each with a decorative-strength and a text-safe deepened variant), Tailwind's slate scale for all neutrals. Deep variants are used anywhere color sits on text or a white button label; raw accents are for decorative fills, large elements, and the mark itself.

Mark

The flower mark

Personal mark, redrawn as a clean vector from a hand-drawn original (five overlapping circles, pinwheel-style occlusion so every petal is equally exposed, organic-width outline). Component motifs below are all pulled from its parts: the dot, the stamen spray, the organic variable-width line.

Static

Narrative

Plain flowing prose, used for Problem/Role and Before/After framing on every case study. Replaced an earlier two-column grid pattern (Problem beside Role, Before beside After) that implied a false point-by-point correlation between columns that weren't actually parallel. One or two paragraphs, no visual structure beyond the text itself.

Security teams found problems that IT teams manually translated into fixes — slow, fragmented, coordination-heavy. Today, the agent converts vulnerability intelligence into remediation plans, ready for admin review and approval.

Static

Pull quote

One standout statement (usually a concrete outcome or metric) pulled out of the surrounding text and given visual weight — large serif italic with a coral rule. Used sparingly, at most once or twice per case study, for the "show don't over-explain" principle.

An early preview customer collapsed a two-week remediation cycle to under two minutes.

Static

Image placeholder

Plain gray box (.img-ph), deliberately unstyled as a "design element" — no dashed border, no brand color, no caption required — so it never gets mistaken for a finished part of the page. Swap for a real photo, sketch, whiteboard shot, or screenshot by replacing the element with an <img>. .case-cover combines this with a 21:9 aspect ratio for the large image at the top of each case study.
